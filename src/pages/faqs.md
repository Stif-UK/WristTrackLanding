# Frequently Asked Questions

### What is WristTrack?

WristTrack is a dedicated watch collection log, daily wear tracker, and accuracy monitor built specifically for watch enthusiasts by a fellow collector. It replaces messy spreadsheets and generic note-taking apps with a clean, purpose-built companion to catalogue your timepieces, analyse your wrist-time habits, and keep tabs on how your movements are performing.

There are no artificial limits on how many watches you can add for free - whether you own a modest three watch rotation or a sprawling vault of three hundred vintage grails, you can track your entire collection right out of the box.

### Tracking watches

Logging your wrist time is quick and straightforward:

1. **Add your watches:** Catalogue your timepieces with key details including brand, model, reference number, movement type, purchase date, case dimensions, and lug width, alongside high-resolution photos for your gallery.
2. **Log daily wear:** Each day, simply tap the watch record you're wearing and click 'wear today' (you can do this on as many watches as you like - there is no limit on how many watches you can wear in a day...) You can also backdate entries if you are importing historical wear data.
3. **Analyse your habits:** Head to the stats tab to group your charts by brand, movement type, or watch category, and filter across both preset and custom date ranges to see what genuinely gets wrist time—and what stays in the watch box.

### Time Settings

WristTrack features built-in **atomic time synchronisation** so you can set your mechanical, automatic, and quartz watches with split-second precision. Rather than relying on your device's internal system clock (which can drift) WristTrack queries accurate network time servers to give you a true atomic reference right on your screen.

### Accuracy Setting

Mechanical watches naturally gain or lose a few seconds each day. WristTrack’s accuracy tracker lets you measure and record the daily rate (**+/- seconds per day, month, or year**) of your movements over time:

1. Synchronise your watch against the built-in atomic clock.
2. Check back hours, days, or weeks later and log the offset in WristTrack.
3. Review your historical accuracy logs to spot sudden changes in amplitude or rate—helping you know exactly when a watch is running within specification and when it might be due for a service.

### How many watches can I track? Are there limits to the analytics data I can see?

As many as you want! I have noticed many apps reel you in with the ability to track three or six watches, forcing you to pay if you want to track more, but I believe that artificially limiting the number of watches that can be tracked destroys the value of the data - as an enthusiast I want to be able to track my whole collection, not a part of it!
Likewise, I have seen other apps that only let you see your last 6 months of data unless you sign up for a subscription, but again to me this destroys the value of the app instantly.

Instead WristTrack gives you the ability to add your entire collection for free, and see all of the data you have tracked. There is a donation system that supports the apps development, but instead of crippling the free option to drive sales, WristTrack takes the approach of simply adding _more value_ for those that donate (and specifically increasing the amount of data that can be tracked) - I don't want to force _all users_ to upgrade, I want to add useful additional insights and information for enthusiasts that will find value from it - see [What are the benefits of WristTrack Pro?](#what-are-the-benefits-of-wristtrack-pro)

### How is WristTrack funded? Will you sell my data?

I think this is an important question to ask of any app - especially free ones. If there is no clear monetisation on a free app then chances are _you are the product_, with your data being sold to data brokers (especially on apps that insist on logging in for use).

WristTrack **does not sell user data**. Logging in is entirely optional - you can use the app offline with no account if you like, and your data never leaves your device.

If you do choose to login your collection data is securely encrypted and stored in the cloud, but the app remains _local first_ (see [Why does data take a few moments to sync between my devices?](#why-does-data-take-a-few-moments-to-sync-between-my-devices) below) and fully functional offline.

WristTrack is funded by small ads (banners, never overlays!), WristTrack Pro donations and any income generated by the [WristTrack merch store](https://wristtrack.teemill.com).

When I first created WristTrack I simply wanted to earn enough to cover my annual Apple £79 fee - now I channel any additional improvements to making WristTrack better (for example the income from WristTrack goes into hosting this site, running the backend servers that allow cloud sync and the purchase of physical devices to improve the quality of testing new releases - for this year I bought an old 2022 iPhone SE, an old 10" Samsung Tablet and an iPad Mini, which have all contributed to the development of WristTrack 2.0!)

Overall though WristTrack remains relatively cheap to run - it doesn't need a heavy monetisation strategy as I am careful about adding features that are affordable. You won't find any social network features or unlimited watch images, because these can become very expensive, very quickly.

Instead WristTrack focuses on adding features that add value to watch collectors - such as tracking details of your collection and empowering you to utilise that data.

### What does WristTrack do with my data

The way WristTrack uses data is laid out in the apps [privacy policy](/privacypolicy), however to keep things simple - if you login to enable cloud sync, then your data is securely copied to WristTrack's back end servers.

As mentioned above, **WristTrack does not sell user data**.

Data can be used in aggregate to generate community level insights (no personal data is used for this, and no private fields such as watch notes are ever aggregated) - for example, data pulled into an anonymised analytics table can allow WristTrack to calculate the most worn brands across the community in a particular month or the total wears tracked over a year - fun insights!

### Why does WristTrack show ads

WristTrack is developed and maintained by a sole independent developer rather than a venture-backed software company. Showing non-intrusive banner ads in the free version helps cover essential running costs - such as Apple Developer and Google Play fees, domain hosting, and cloud infrastructure bills - so the core app can remain completely free with no collection caps.

Upgrading to **WristTrack Pro** removes ads entirely while unlocking additional enthusiast features.

* **Multi-Timezone GMT Tracker:** Keep tabs on home and local times effortlessly—ideal for setting caller or flyer GMTs and tracking multiple time zones at a glance.
* **Additional Horological Data Points:** Unlock deeper specification fields (such as water resistance, case dimensions, power reserve, and service history notes), and expanded analytics for serious collectors.

* **100% Reinvested:** Every donation goes directly towards keeping the lights on and funding new features and improvements.
* **No Recurring Trap:** Your Pro unlock is not tied to an ongoing subscription. Down the line, the app may show a gentle, occasional reminder inviting you to consider making another voluntary contribution if you continue to get value from it - with zero obligation and no features revoked if you choose not to.

### Is WristTrack "vibe coded"?

No. WristTrack first launched in **2022** as a personal hobby project, built from the ground up with traditional software engineering practices long before generative AI coding became commonplace.

Today, modern AI tools are used as part of the development workflow to help speed up routine tasks and small, straightforward changes. However, **every single line of code is carefully reviewed, tested, and understood by a human developer** before it ever reaches your device.

Purely "vibe coded" apps - where software is blindly generated without human oversight - carry legitimate risks for users, including:
* **Data fragility and loss:** Unverified database schemas and sync logic can silently corrupt or overwrite years of personal collection records.
* **Security and privacy gaps:** AI-generated backend rules frequently leave user data exposed or improperly authenticated.
* **Unmaintainable codebases:** Software built like a house of cards quickly breaks when adding new features or updating for new iOS and Android OS releases.

WristTrack is engineered to last, treating your collection data with the same care and reliability you expect from a well-built mechanical movement.

### Why does data take a few moments to sync between my devices?

WristTrack uses an **offline-first** design. Any changes you make—such as tracking a wear date—are saved immediately to your local device so you can use the app seamlessly, even without an internet connection.

When updating data across multiple devices (such as your phone and tablet):

1. **Cloud upload:** Your first device updates its local data instantly and sends the changes to the cloud in the background.
2. **Real-time update:** Your second device detects the change in the cloud and automatically downloads the new data.

If your second device initially shows as **"Up to date"** but still displays older data for a moment, it simply means it checked the cloud right before the upload from your first device finished, or it is re-establishing its background connection after being opened.

Your data is completely safe, and your second device will update automatically within a few moments.
